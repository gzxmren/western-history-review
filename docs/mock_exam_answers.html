Question 1 (10 marks)

(a) The Third Estate paid all the taxes. (2 marks)

(b) The First Estate (Clergy) and the Second Estate (Nobility) paid no taxes. (2 marks — 1 each)

(c) Model answer (6 marks):

Source A shows that the Third Estate (97% of the population) bore all the tax burden while the clergy and nobility paid nothing. This social inequality was a major cause of the French Revolution.

In my own knowledge, the Third Estate included peasants, workers, and the bourgeoisie (middle class). The bourgeoisie were educated and wealthy but had no political power. They were influenced by Enlightenment ideas (e.g., Locke's natural rights, Rousseau's social contract) and began to question why they should accept this unfair system. Economic hardship — bad harvests and rising bread prices — made the situation worse. The Third Estate demanded equality and a fairer system, which led to the Revolution of 1789.

Mark breakdown: (a) 2 marks for correct group. (b) 2 marks (1 each for clergy and nobility). (c) 6 marks — 2 for using Source A evidence, 2 for own knowledge (Enlightenment ideas, economic factors), 2 for clear explanation linking to the Revolution.

Question 2 (12 marks)

Model answer:

Impact 1: Child Labour (6 marks)
Source B states that "children as young as six or seven" worked in coal mines and cotton factories for "fourteen to sixteen hours a day." This shows that child labour was widespread during the Industrial Revolution. In my own knowledge, factory owners hired children because they could be paid less than adults, and their small hands were useful for working with machines. Children received no education and suffered serious health problems from long working hours and dangerous conditions. This was a major negative social impact of the Industrial Revolution.

Impact 2: Poor Working and Living Conditions (6 marks)
Source B mentions "crowded, unsanitary rooms" and dangerous machines where "accidents are common." Workers who were injured were often "dismissed without pay." In my own knowledge, the factory system created a new working class that lived in overcrowded cities with poor sanitation. Diseases spread quickly. There were no labour laws to protect workers — they had no job security, no safety regulations, and no compensation for injuries.

Mark breakdown: 6 marks per impact (×2 = 12 marks). Each impact: 2 marks for evidence from source, 2 marks for own knowledge, 2 marks for clear explanation.

Question 3 (12 marks)

(a) According to Locke, all men have the rights to life, liberty, and property. (3 marks — 1 each)

(b) If the government fails to protect these rights, the people have the right to overthrow it and establish a new government. (2 marks)

(c) Model answer (7 marks):

Locke's ideas about natural rights directly challenged the absolute monarchy in France. The French king, Louis XVI, ruled by divine right, meaning he claimed his authority came from God. Locke argued that government authority comes from the people, not God, and that its purpose is to protect people's rights — not to take them away.

Other Enlightenment thinkers added to this. Rousseau proposed the social contract — an agreement between people and government. If the government breaks the contract, the people can remove it. Montesquieu proposed the separation of powers to prevent any one person from having too much power, which was a direct criticism of the French monarchy.

These ideas spread among the educated Third Estate (the bourgeoisie). When France faced a financial crisis and people were suffering from food shortages, they began to ask: "Why should we obey a king who does not protect our rights?" The answer was the French Revolution of 1789, which overthrew the monarchy and established a republic based on the principles of liberty, equality, and fraternity.

Mark breakdown: (a) 3 marks (1 per right). (b) 2 marks. (c) 7 marks — 2 for discussing Locke's ideas, 2 for including other Enlightenment thinkers (Rousseau, Montesquieu), 3 for explaining clearly how these ideas connected to the French Revolution.

Question 4 (10 marks)

Model answer:

I agree that the Scientific Revolution was very important because it changed the way Europeans thought, but I do not think it was the only important development.

The Scientific Revolution introduced rational thinking and the scientific method. Newton's Clockwork Universe Theory showed that the universe follows fixed laws. This encouraged Europeans to use reason rather than blindly accepting traditional authority — including the Church.

This new way of thinking directly led to the Enlightenment, where thinkers applied rational thinking to society and government. Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu proposed ideas about natural rights, social contract, and separation of powers. These ideas then inspired the French Revolution of 1789.

The Scientific Revolution also prepared the ground for the Industrial Revolution. Scientific theories about physics, chemistry, and engineering were applied to practical use — for example, Newton's laws helped in the development of machines. Watt's steam engine was a result of applying scientific knowledge to technology.

However, other developments were equally important. The Industrial Revolution transformed Europe's economy and made it technologically supreme. The French Revolution spread the ideas of democracy and human rights. Without these, Europe would not have risen to global dominance.

Conclusion: The Scientific Revolution was the starting point that changed European thinking and enabled later developments. However, it was the combination of all these revolutions — Scientific, Enlightenment, Industrial, and French — that together shaped the rise of modern Europe.

Mark breakdown: 10 marks total. 2 marks for stating a clear position (agree/disagree/partially agree). 2 marks for explaining the importance of the Scientific Revolution. 4 marks for discussing two other developments (Enlightenment, Industrial Rev, French Rev). 2 marks for a reasoned conclusion.
