Section E — Data-based Questions (44 marks)

Study the sources below and answer the questions that follow. Remember to use both the sources and your own knowledge.

Source A

The following is an excerpt from a pamphlet written by Abbé Sieyès in 1789, just before the French Revolution:

"What is the Third Estate? Everything. What has it been in the political order until now? Nothing. What does it ask for? To become something. … The Third Estate bears all the burdens of the state. The nobles enjoy privileges without contributing anything. The Church owns vast lands and collects tithes but pays no taxes. Is it not time to change this unjust system?"

— Adapted from Abbé Sieyès, What is the Third Estate? (1789)

Source B

The following is a description of factory conditions during the Industrial Revolution in Britain, written by a government inspector in 1842:

"Children as young as six or seven years old work in the coal mines and cotton factories. They work fourteen to sixteen hours a day, six days a week. The air is filled with coal dust and cotton fibres. The machines are dangerous, and accidents are common. Workers who are injured are often dismissed without pay. Many families live in crowded, unsanitary rooms near the factories."

— Adapted from reports of the Children's Employment Commission (1842)

Source C

The following is a statement by Enlightenment thinker John Locke:

"All men are born with certain natural rights — the rights to life, liberty, and property. The purpose of government is to protect these rights. If a government fails to do so, or if it violates these rights, the people have the right to overthrow it and establish a new government."

— Based on John Locke, Two Treatises of Government (1689)
